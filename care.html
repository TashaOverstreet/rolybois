Care Sheet

Step 1: Using spring or distilled water, moisten the substrate. You want the moisture level to be damp but not soaked or wet. You should be able to squeeze it like a sponge and not have any extra water come out while still feeling damp.


Step 2: Spread the substrate evenly over the bottom of the container. Do not pack it down. The substrate should remain loose.


Step 3: Using spring or distilled water, moisten the sphagnum moss. Squeeze like a sponge to expel any excess water. Place the moss on the back 1/3 of the substrate. This will be the moist side of the colony.


Step 4: Spread the leaf litter evenly over the substrate that is not covered in sphagnum moss.


Step 5: Place the wood hide on top of the leaf litter. Place the decoration piece on the dry side opposite the sphagnum moss. Place the cuttlebone on the dry side as well.


Step 6: Add your isopods! Have fun and enjoy.


Remember to check the moisture levels every 3 to 5 days and adjust as needed. I like to feed mine carrots and raw sweet potatoes since they do not mold as quickly. Dried shrimp is a good source of protein. Fish flakes are also a cheap and effective source of protein, but they mold quickly. Only use as much as the colony can consume within a 24 hour period when using a food source that creates mold quickly.
